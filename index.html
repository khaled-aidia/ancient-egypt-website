Predynastic

(c. 5000-3100 B.C.)

Predynastic

There have been few periods in history that have left behind as many indelible marks on human culture as Ancient Egypt.

sphinx

Neolithic (late Stone Age) communities in northeastern Africa exchanged hunting for agriculture and made early advances that paved the way for the later development.

Civilization

Around 3400 B.C., two separate kingdoms were established near the Fertile Crescent, an area home to some of the world’s oldest civilizations: the Red Land.

Egyptians

A southern king, Scorpion, made the first attempts to conquer the northern kingdom around 3200 B.C. A century later, King Menes would subdue the north.

Archaic

(c. 3100-2686 B.C.)

Archaeology

King Menes founded the capital of ancient Egypt at White Walls (later known as Memphis), in the north, near the apex of the Nile River delta.

Museums

The capital would grow into a great metropolis that dominated Egyptian society during the Old Kingdom period.

Rescue

The Archaic Period saw the development of the foundations of Egyptian society, including the all-important ideology of kingship.

Kingdom

(c. 2686-2181 B.C.)

The Old Kingdom began with the third dynasty of pharaohs. Around 2630 B.C., the third dynasty’s King Djoser asked Imhotep, an architect, priest and healer, to design a funerary monument for him.

the result was the world’s first major stone building, the Step-Pyramid at Saqqara, near Memphis. Egyptian pyramid-building reached its zenith with the construction of the Great Pyramid at Giza, on the outskirts of Cairo.

The Gods

Divine Rulers of Ancient Egypt

Ra (Sun God)

The supreme sun god, creator of the universe and giver of life. Believed to sail across the sky daily in his solar bark.

Anubis

The jackal-headed god of embalming and the dead. He guided souls through the underworld and weighed their hearts.

Osiris

God of the afterlife, death, and resurrection. Murdered by his brother Set but revived to rule the underworld.

Pharaohs

The Living Gods on Earth

Ramses II

Often regarded as the greatest, most celebrated, and most powerful pharaoh of the New Kingdom.

Tutankhamun

The boy king whose nearly intact tomb was discovered in 1922, providing unprecedented insights into royal life.

Hatshepsut

One of the few female pharaohs, overseeing a period of incredible wealth, monumental building, and trade expansion.
